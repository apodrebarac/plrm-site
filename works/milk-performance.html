Work 02

Milk Performance

Year March 2018
Location Central St Martins, London
Medium Endurance performance, 30 minutes
Status Complete

Paradox of nourishment. Paradox of the body.

Milk, traditionally a symbol of nourishment, care, and motherhood, became a site of contradiction within the body. Living with illness, the body produced milk even though it could not sustain life — yet when ingested, the body rejected it violently. The performance exposed a paradox between the desire to nurture and the material impossibility of doing so, culminating in physical collapse and loss of consciousness.

What should have nourished instead became an agent of harm, revealing the tension between cultural symbolism and lived bodily reality. A 30-minute endurance piece in which milk was drunk despite the body's inability to process it. The act of defiance against physical limitations induced intense pain and hardship. As the milk was forced into the system, the body reacted violently, ultimately ejecting it and causing collapse from the excruciating pain.

This performance was not about the physical act of drinking milk. It was an exploration of the agony and the relentless struggle against one's own bodily limitations. The duration emphasised the torturous experience — the slow, agonising process of enduring and then succumbing to the pain.

Methodological significance

Contradiction is a lived condition. The body in this performance held mutually incompatible truths without resolution, demonstrating that meaning and knowledge emerge from inhabiting tension rather than resolving it. The Milk Performance became an epistemic ground for PLRM, showing that the body generates understanding through enduring the paradoxes it cannot reconcile.

The performance goes beyond physical sensations to touch on deep emotional and psychological levels. The battle against one's own body, against illness, the pain endured, and the ultimate surrender to physical limitations created a narrative of vulnerability and resilience.
