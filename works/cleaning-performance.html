Work 05

Cleaning Performance

Year January 2019
Medium Durational performance
Subtitle Slowness, Care, and the Ethics of Aftermath
Documentation Author

Insight emerges in the aftermath of rupture. Care is its own kind of knowledge.

The Cleaning Performance emerged as a direct response to the failure of Guilt, Pain, Chained. Instead of staging pain, I turned to the slow and attentive labour of cleaning blood and honey from one hundred chain links. Performed quietly and methodically, I repositioned the act of after-care as the central performative act — not the wound, but its tending.

Each chain link required individual attention. The process was slow, repetitive, and without spectacle. The audience, if present, witnessed not suffering but its aftermath — the patient work of returning something marked to a state of care. The honey and blood, mixed together, required different techniques to remove: some residue dissolved with patience, some had to be worked at repeatedly.

The work reframed what counts as performance. Rather than the intensity of action at a peak moment, it proposed the durational labour of maintenance — the unglamorous, necessary work that follows rupture and makes continuation possible.

Methodological significance

This performance revealed a key methodological principle within PLRM: insight emerges in the aftermath of rupture. The act of care became the place where knowledge materialised, showing that transformation is often found in the quiet, secondary gestures rather than in the intensity of primary action. This shifted PLRM's focus from the aesthetics of endurance toward the ethics of care — a shift that would inform all subsequent computational and relational work.
