Work 05

Guilt, Pain & Chained

Year December 2018 – 2019
Location Central St Martins, London
Medium Endurance performance, chains, honey
Weight Performance I: 53kg / Performance II: 67kg
Status Complete

I run on guilt. Guilty: because I don't take care of my health. Frantic: because I haven't realized all of my goals and find myself getting further and further away from being able to accept love in my life. In my frenzy to be alive and heard, I'm afraid I may not really be experiencing my life.

— Ron Athey

This body of work centres on themes of endurance and durational performance, exploring the depths of physical and mental resilience. In these performances, the body is subjected to the weight and captivity of heavy chains — symbolising the struggle and ultimate loss of control. This exploration is driven by a profound need to push the body to its limits, to endure pain and suffering, and to transcend the boundaries of physical and emotional endurance.

Performance I

The first performance utilised 12-metre-long chains weighing 53 kilograms, coated with honey to reflect the Renaissance tradition of depicting the tortured body in spectacle. The honey — a symbol of prosperity — was intended to transform suffering into a metaphorical quest for immortality and abundance.

Performance II

The second performance involved 18 metres of chain, weighing 67 kilograms, knotted in a complex pattern that ultimately trapped the performer. This act of chaining was a test of endurance and stillness, pushing the body to withstand immense weight. A lapse in focus led to a mistake in untying the knots, heightening the experience of extreme suffering. It left bruising and required several people to release the performer from the knot of chains.

Reflection

The aftermath left a reckoning with inadequate research and preparation. The intention to communicate a message of resistance and the limitations of the human body was overshadowed by the aesthetic value of the performance, which failed to transcend into meaningful language. The documentation revealed a cathartic experience — an event that paradoxically released the very pain and torture it sought to express.

The notion of "useless suffering" as described by Emmanuel Levinas — where pain's denial of meaning compels a response — resonates with this work. Through these performances, the balance between the physical and the immaterial, the brute reality of the body, and its role as a conduit for cultural expression continued to be explored.
