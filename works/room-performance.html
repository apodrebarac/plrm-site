Work 04

Room Performance

Year 2019
Medium Live performance, video installation, two-room
Status Complete

Containment, rupture, and the threshold of pain.

Trauma fractures the unity of body and mind, and afterwards neither memory nor perception can be trusted. In this intermediate realm, our binary system of understanding ties erratically between helplessness and rage, breakdown and unity. It is in this space of incoherence where this performance emerges, outlining the raw sensations of trauma as they inscribe themselves upon the body.

The performance unfolded across two interconnected spaces that together articulated a movement from containment to rupture. In the first room, the performer remained enclosed, silent, and internally recursive, holding a tension that could not yet find form. This claustrophobic state accumulated pressure within the body. When the performance shifted into the second room — projected, magnified, and made visible — the accumulated strain broke outward.

Through the repetitive act of knocking a hand against the wall until the skin split and bled, the performance enacted a rupture that the enclosed space could not contain. A video camera captured the essence of this rupture — the struggle between fragmentation and the pursuit of unity. Each movement maps the mental imprints of terror: the visceral experience of shaking, the loss of control over one's physical form, the loss of identity and memory.

Installation

The videos loop endlessly — a fragmented self caught in the relentless circle of its own broken narratives. A life-sized projection of the performer pacing the room was installed alongside a live performance, creating an unsettling resonance between physical presence and its digital double. During the performance, the mechanical knocking on the wall grew increasingly loud. Caught in the rhythmic compulsion, the performance only stopped when the hand was noticed to be bleeding — a physical manifestation of the internal rupture.

Methodological significance

This transition revealed a foundational methodological principle within PLRM: what cannot be processed internally will eventually force its way through the body. In this work, pain became a vocabulary for the unspeakable — a means through which contradiction and emotional residue found expression. Room Performance marks a moment when the body shifted from subject to epistemic instrument, showing that rupture is a necessary form of making visible what cannot remain silent.
