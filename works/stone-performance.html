Work 01

Stone Performance

Year November 2017
Location Central St Martins, London
Medium Endurance performance, AI sound composition
Status Complete

Matter as resistance. Resistance as thought.

The performance explores themes of duration, pain, and the fear of being struck. Beginning with stones as objects within a space, photography was used to extract and interpret their intrinsic information. By collecting concrete, man-made rocks, audio processing software and artificial neural networks converted the data from these stones into a sound composition, establishing patterns and rhythms that mirrored their essence.

A durational performance unfolded in which stones were repeatedly thrown and struck against each other until they disintegrated into dust. This rigorous act demanded intense physical energy and concentration, highlighting the interplay between two organic bodies: the performer's and the stones'.

The transformation from solid stones to digital files and then to dust symbolises a cycle of death and rebirth across physical and digital realms. By converting photographic data and manipulating it within a machine, the work ventures into the realms of modelling and extracting data from non-human forms, pushing the boundaries of what counts as artistic subject matter.

Methodological significance

This work gave PLRM its earliest insight: that repetition generates transformation, not sameness, and that material thinking emerges through friction. With each impact, a micro-history forms — the force of the strike, the vibration through the arm, the dust released into air. The stone becomes a collaborator in thought, revealing what breaks first, what yields, what endures. The dust is its data — a material trace of recursive action.

Audience

The visceral nature of the stone performance transforms pain into a multi-faceted sensory experience. The repetitive act of striking stones, the sound of each impact, and the physical toll on the performer's body create a palpable sense of tension. The audience is not merely observers but are drawn into the visceral reality of the performance — the anticipation of each strike and the resulting physical and emotional strain on the performer evoking a profound empathetic response, making the pain felt in the performance almost tangible.
