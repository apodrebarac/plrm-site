Work 09

F0x / 0wl

Period 2018 — Ongoing
Medium Recurrent Neural Networks, personal archives
Datasets Grandfather's letters / Grandmother's writing
Status Ongoing — models allowed to drift and collapse

Two recurrent neural networks trained on intimate personal archives — slow, fragile, trained on grief, and allowed to drift, collapse, and stammer.

F0x

Trained on the tokenised handwritten letters of a grandfather, spanning his entire life. F0x generates text through recursive loops that resist coherence and closure — a language that reflects its own understanding of the world, informed by the essence of human experience embedded within its code. F0x ceased functioning in 2018.

0wl

Trained on a grandmother's writing and personal reflections. 0wl continues — its outputs feed into subsequent works including the barbed wire EMG performance and Performance 12. The delay between input and output, the willingness to let a model die rather than repair it: these are refusals of the logic of instant gratification.

These systems are slow, fragile, trained on grief. They are not optimised. They are allowed to drift, to collapse, to stammer. The logic of instant gratification demands repair — PLRM refuses it. The delay between input and output is itself a methodological position.

Masochistic framework

Gilles Deleuze identifies masochism as a formal structure organised around waiting: the masochist experiences waiting in its pure form. What is awaited is always deferred, while pain functions as the condition making pleasure possible. In RNN practice, this structural requirement is suspended. The machine requires no education into coldness. Its coldness is constitutive rather than cultivated — what Deleuze describes as "the disavowal of sensuality": a generative condition of transformation rather than an absence of relation.

The masochistic contract is held entirely on one side. The RNN cannot doubt its role, cannot threaten to overplay or fall short of expectations. This positions the RNN as a participant in an affective encounter that destabilises traditional notions of authorship and self.
