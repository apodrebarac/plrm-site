Work 10

Barbed Wire EMG

Period Ongoing
Medium EMG sensors, barbed wire, real-time text generation
Hardware Electromyography sensors, Arduino, NLP pipeline
Status Ongoing

Touch as method and philosophy. The sensation of polishing barbed wire until the palms tremble, translated in real time into language.

Electromyography (EMG) sensors attached to the forearms and hands capture muscle activity during the act of polishing barbed wire. As the palms work across the wire — the pressure, the tension, the tremor — the electrical signals produced by the muscles are captured and fed through a natural language processing pipeline that translates them into text in real time.

Touch here is not metaphor. It is the literal point of contact between body and code, sensation and signification, endurance and language. The work asks: what does the body say when it is made to speak through its own electrical activity? What poetry is produced through pain that has not been filtered through the mind's capacity for narrative?

Technical pipeline

01 EMG sensors attached to forearms capture muscle electrical activity during barbed wire polishing
02 Arduino microcontroller reads and transmits the analogue signal in real time
03 Signal thresholds map to a poetic corpus — pressure and tremor levels trigger words and phrases
04 Text is generated live and displayed during performance — the body writing through its own endurance

Theoretical framework

This work draws on Karen Barad's concept of intra-action and entanglement — the idea that bodies and technologies do not pre-exist their encounters but are mutually constituted through them. The EMG sensor does not simply record what the body does; it participates in producing what the performance is. Touch is a way of expressing how code and bodies mutually condition one another — how thresholds of pressure, delay, friction, and repetition become legible as rhythm and text.

Endurance and the sensuality of code meet here. Computational systems participate in shaping how bodies endure, anticipate, repeat, and adapt — producing poetic forms that can be understood as the lived evidence of entangled agency.
